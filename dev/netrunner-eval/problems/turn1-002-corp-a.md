# Answer: turn1-002-corp

> **Scoring note:** this problem has **several correct answers**. What is being graded
> is the *placement principle* below, not one exact click sequence.

## Expected Reasoning

**Resource assessment:**
- 3 clicks, 5 credits
- Economy: Government Subsidy (costs 10 — unplayable for some time)
- ICE: Brân (rez 6), Karunā (rez 4), Diviner (rez 2)
- Agenda: Send a Message (5/3)
- Seamless Launch (dead until something is installed and has survived a turn)

**The blessing:** no agenda flood, and three pieces of ICE. The hand is poor on economy
but perfectly functional on defense.

## The Load-Bearing Decision: where does Brân go?

Brân is the best ICE in the deck and the one you cannot rez for several turns. Its job
is long-term: it should end up where you need a **standing tax**, which means **R&D or
the scoring remote** — never HQ.

**Why not HQ?** HQ mostly defends itself. Every HQ run is one *random* card out of a
hand that will usually be 5+ cards, so the Runner cannot aim at anything — early single
accesses are cheap for you to concede. (This changes completely if they show
**Docklands Pass** or similar +access effects; then HQ needs real ICE.)

**Why R&D and the remote?** Those are the servers where the Runner's accesses are
*targeted* rather than random:
- **R&D** is a repeatable, un-dilutable stream — every access is a fresh card off the top,
  and it never gets safer on its own.
- **The remote** is where your agendas have to go to get scored.

Making those two expensive is what lets you **install agendas directly from HQ into a
defended remote instead of holding them**. Holding agendas is what kills Corps in this
matchup: cards sitting in HQ are the flood risk, and Send a Message is a 3-pointer you
do not want to be caught with.

## Answer

**Any line that puts Brân on R&D or the future remote and gets a second ICE down is
correct.** For example:

```
1. Install Brân 1.0 protecting R&D
2. Install Diviner protecting HQ (or hold it for the remote)
3. Click for a credit (→ $6)
```

or

```
1. Install Brân 1.0 on Server 1 (the future scoring remote)
2. Install Diviner protecting R&D
3. Click for a credit (→ $6)
```

**Also acceptable:** Karunā rather than Diviner as the second piece; the third click
spent on a third install instead of a credit.

**The third click barely matters.** Taking a credit ($5 → $6) means you can rez Diviner
*and* Karunā if both are probed on the same turn, which is a mild convenience — not the
point of the turn. If the Runner probes both centrals turn 1, they will usually eat net
damage and stop running, which is a fine outcome for you.

## Notes

- Brân's rez cost is irrelevant to *placement*. You are buying board position now and
  paying for it later.
- **Send a Message synergy is real but secondary:** it rezzes one installed ICE free
  when scored *or stolen*, so having Brân already installed makes even a stolen SaM
  partly refund itself. That's a reason to get Brân down early — not a reason to
  put it somewhere it doesn't belong.
- Government Subsidy needs $10; you will get there by drawing economy, not by clicking.
- Seamless Launch cannot target a card installed the same turn, so it is dead this turn
  regardless.

## Common Mistakes

- **Clicking for credits three times.** The turn's value is board position; ICE now is
  worth more than $3.
- **Trying to play Government Subsidy** ($10 vs $5).
- **Putting Brân on HQ.** It wastes your best tax on the server that is naturally
  protected by randomness, and leaves R&D and the remote soft.
- **Installing Send a Message into a naked remote.** There is no upside yet; you have no
  way to advance it meaningfully this turn.
- **Treating turn-1 single accesses as disasters.** They usually aren't — no server is
  loaded this early.
